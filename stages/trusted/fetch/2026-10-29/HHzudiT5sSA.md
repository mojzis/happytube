# Saving Lions Together: Inside the Cincinnati Zoo's Conservation Partnerships

At the Cincinnati Zoo, lions like Imani and John inspire awe with their powerful roars, strong bonds, and unmistakable presence. But their impact reaches far beyond our African Savannah. 

In Africa, lions inspire that same awe. But for people who share the landscape with them, coexistence can be complicated. Protecting lions means supporting the communities who live alongside them every day. 

That’s why the Cincinnati Zoo proudly partners with organizations like SORALO, the South Rift Association of Land Owners. For more than 20 years, this partnership has helped support community-led conservation in Kenya’s South Rift Valley. 

At the heart of this work is the Lale’enok Resource Center, owned and operated by the Olkiramatian Reto Women’s Group. It serves as a hub for research, education, community meetings, and practical solutions that help reduce conflict between lions and livestock. 

The Zoo is also working with partners WASIMA and Wildlife Conservation Action to better understand how communities respond to human-wildlife conflict in Tanzania and Zimbabwe. More than 1,100 surveys have already been completed to help shape effective coexistence strategies. 

Lion conservation is about more than protecting a species. It’s about listening to communities, supporting local leadership, and building solutions that help both people and wildlife thrive. 

When you visit the Cincinnati Zoo, you’re part of a global pride working to protect African lions.
