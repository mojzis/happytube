# Andalas Turns 25: The Sumatran Rhino Calf Who Changed Conservation History

Twenty-five years ago, at the Cincinnati Zoo & Botanical Garden, Andalas made history. He became the first Sumatran rhino calf born in a zoo in over a century. It was a milestone that reshaped the future of one of the world's most endangered species. Born September 13, 2001, Andalas was the result of years of scientific persistence by CREW (Cincinnati Zoo's Center for Conservation and Research of Endangered Wildlife), working closely with rhino keepers to crack the reproductive biology of this rare species. He was later joined by his sister, Suci and brother, Harapan. In 2007, Andalas moved to the Sumatran Rhino Sanctuary in Indonesia's Way Kambas National Park to help lead in-country breeding efforts. Today, his legacy spans generations. With fewer than 50 wild Sumatran rhinos left, SRS and its resident rhinos represent one of the last best hopes for the species' survival. Join Dr. Terri Roth as she reflects on 25 years of scientific breakthroughs, international collaboration and the rhinos whose future still depends on it.
